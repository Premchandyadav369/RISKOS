01

Real-Time Market Intelligence

Continuously monitors global equity, forex, commodity, and fixed-income markets across every timezone. Surfaces structural movements — not headlines.

What it does

Raw price feeds are processed through statistical filters to detect regime changes, volatility shifts, and cross-asset divergences in real time. When a 2σ move in implied volatility occurs while realized volatility stays flat, the system flags it as actionable. Think of it as a radar that watches every market simultaneously and only alerts you when something genuinely unusual happens.

Under the hood

  • EWMA Volatility Estimators — Exponentially weighted moving averages that give more importance to recent price movements, so the volatility estimate reacts quickly to market shocks instead of being anchored to stale data.
  • Hidden Markov Model (HMM) — A probabilistic model that identifies invisible market "states" (bull trend, bear trend, sideways). Transition probabilities are fitted via the Baum-Welch algorithm. In plain terms: it figures out which phase the market is in — even though the phase itself is never directly observable — like detecting the season by looking at the weather.
  • Cross-Correlation Monitors — Tracks how assets move relative to each other in real time. When correlations break from their historical pattern (e.g., stocks and bonds suddenly moving together instead of apart), the system identifies it as a regime shift.
02

Risk & Portfolio Engine

Measures how much your portfolio can lose, builds the optimal allocation to minimize that loss, then validates the model against reality. One unified pipeline: measure → optimize → stress-test → validate.

What it does

Calculates Value at Risk (VaR) and Expected Shortfall (CVaR) using three independent methods — historical simulation, parametric estimation, and Monte Carlo. Then uses CVaR optimization to build portfolios that maximize return for a given risk budget. Finally, replays the strategy against years of historical data (including major crises) and applies hypothetical extreme scenarios to verify everything holds up.

Under the hood

  • GARCH(1,1) Volatility — Models volatility as a time-varying process: σ²ₜ = ω + α·ε²ₜ₋₁ + β·σ²ₜ₋₁. Captures the empirical fact that large returns tend to follow large returns. In plain terms: after a big market move, the next few days tend to be more volatile too. GARCH measures this "momentum of risk" so the system knows when to tighten position sizes.
  • Monte Carlo VaR & CVaR — Generates 10,000+ correlated random scenarios from the estimated return distribution. VaR tells you the boundary of normal bad days ("with 99% confidence, your maximum loss won't exceed ₹X"). CVaR tells you how bad it gets when things go really wrong — the average loss in the worst 1% of scenarios.
  • Ledoit-Wolf Shrinkage — When you have many assets, raw correlation estimates are noisy. Ledoit-Wolf optimally blends the sample covariance matrix with a structured target to reduce estimation error. In plain terms: it "calms down" the noisy correlations so the optimizer doesn't make absurd bets based on statistical noise.
  • CVaR Portfolio Optimization — Traditional Markowitz optimization treats upside and downside equally. CVaR optimization only penalizes the downside — specifically, the expected loss in the worst 5% of scenarios. It takes your assets, the current covariance structure, return forecasts, and your constraints (e.g., "no more than 15% in any single name") and finds the best risk-adjusted allocation.
  • Backtesting — Runs your strategy day-by-day through historical data with realistic transaction costs, slippage, and liquidity constraints. Produces Sharpe ratios, maximum drawdown, and Calmar ratios. Think of it as checking your strategy's resume.
  • Stress Testing — Takes your current portfolio and applies scenarios like "interest rates jump 300bps overnight" or "equity markets drop 40% over 3 months." Each scenario produces a concrete P&L impact. Think of it as the fire drill.
  • Kupiec & Christoffersen Tests — A risk model is useless if it's wrong. Kupiec tests whether the model's VaR breaches match the predicted frequency. Christoffersen additionally checks that breaches aren't clustering together. If your model says "1% of the time" — these tests verify it really is 1%.
03

Signal & Execution

When the market regime shifts and the optimizer generates a new target allocation, the execution layer turns that decision into filled orders — with sub-120ms latency and full post-trade analytics.

What it does

Detects the current market regime via the HMM, selects the appropriate strategy (momentum in trends, mean-reversion in ranges, reduced exposure in high-volatility states), and routes orders through a low-latency pipeline. Every order passes through pre-trade risk checks, smart order routing, and algorithmic execution (VWAP/TWAP). Post-trade, the system records fill quality — comparing the actual price against the signal price — so you always know exactly how much implementation cost you.

Under the hood

  • Regime-Adaptive Strategy Selection — The HMM identifies bull, bear, or sideways states. Each state maps to a different strategy. When the model detects a transition (e.g., trending → choppy), the system rebalances automatically. In plain terms: it knows when to push and when to pull back, without you having to guess.
  • Pre-Trade Risk Gate — Every order passes through position limits, concentration limits, and margin checks in under 5ms. If it fails, the order is rejected before reaching the market.
  • Smart Order Routing — Routes to the optimal venue based on spread, depth, and recent fill rates.
  • VWAP / TWAP Execution — Large orders are split across time to minimize market impact. VWAP targets the volume-weighted average price; TWAP spreads evenly across a time window.
  • Post-Trade Analytics — Compares the fill price against the signal price to measure implementation shortfall. In plain terms: it makes sure the gap between "what the model wanted" and "what actually happened" is as small as physically possible.
