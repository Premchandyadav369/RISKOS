Six core capabilities engineered for institutional-grade financial intelligence. Each one built to solve a real problem.

Real-Time Market Intelligence

Continuously monitors global equity, forex, commodity, and fixed-income markets across every timezone. Raw price feeds are processed through statistical filters to surface regime changes, volatility shifts, and cross-asset divergences — not news headlines, but the structural movements beneath them.

How it works

Live market data streams through a pipeline of EWMA volatility estimators, Bollinger regime classifiers, and cross-correlation monitors. When a statistically significant deviation is detected — for example, a 2σ move in implied volatility while realized volatility stays flat — the system flags it as an actionable signal. Think of it as a radar that watches every market simultaneously and only alerts you when something genuinely unusual happens.

Portfolio Risk Engine

Measures how much your portfolio can lose under adverse conditions using three independent methods: Historical simulation, parametric estimation, and Monte Carlo. Goes beyond basic VaR to calculate Expected Shortfall (CVaR) — the average loss in the worst-case scenarios, which is what regulators and serious risk desks actually care about.

How it works

Your portfolio returns are modelled using a GARCH(1,1) volatility process that captures volatility clustering — the fact that large moves tend to follow large moves. The covariance matrix between assets is estimated using the Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage method, which prevents estimation errors from blowing up in large portfolios. Monte Carlo then simulates 10,000+ future scenarios. The result: a daily number that says "with 99% confidence, your maximum loss won't exceed ₹X" — and a CVaR number that says "but if it does exceed that, expect it to be around ₹Y."

Adaptive Signal Generation

Detects whether the market is in a trending, mean-reverting, or high-volatility regime and automatically adjusts the trading strategy. Instead of using one fixed model, the system switches between multiple quantitative strategies depending on what the data says right now.

How it works

A Hidden Markov Model (HMM) runs continuously on price data, identifying the current market "state" — bull trend, bear trend, or sideways chop. Each state has its own optimal strategy: momentum in trending markets, mean-reversion in range-bound markets, and reduced exposure in high-volatility states. When the HMM detects a regime transition (e.g., from trending to choppy), the system rebalances automatically. In simple terms: it knows when to push and when to pull back, without you having to guess.

Optimal Portfolio Construction

Builds portfolios that maximize return for a given risk budget using CVaR optimization — not the traditional mean-variance approach that breaks down in real markets. Handles constraints like maximum position sizes, sector limits, and turnover caps that institutional mandates require.

How it works

Classical Markowitz optimization minimizes variance, but variance treats upside and downside equally — which makes no sense. CVaR optimization instead minimizes your expected loss in the worst 5% of scenarios, which is what you actually want to protect against. The optimizer takes your universe of assets, the current covariance structure, return forecasts from the factor model, and your constraints (e.g., "no more than 15% in any single name, at least 20% in fixed income") and finds the allocation that gives you the best risk-adjusted return. In plain language: it tells you exactly how much to put where, and why.

Stress Testing & Backtesting

Replays your strategy against years of historical data to see how it would have performed — including during major crises. Then applies hypothetical extreme scenarios (rate spikes, credit contagion, flash crashes) to see how your current portfolio would survive conditions that haven't happened yet.

How it works

The backtester runs your strategy day-by-day through historical data with realistic transaction costs, slippage, and liquidity constraints — no hindsight bias. It produces Sharpe ratios, maximum drawdown, Calmar ratios, and rolling performance metrics. The stress tester then takes your current portfolio and applies scenarios like "what if interest rates jump 300bps overnight" or "what if equity markets drop 40% over 3 months." Each scenario produces a concrete P&L impact. Think of backtesting as checking your strategy's resume, and stress testing as its fire drill.

Low-Latency Execution Pipeline

When a signal fires, every millisecond matters. The execution layer turns decisions into orders with sub-120ms latency, using smart order routing, VWAP/TWAP algorithms, and real-time slippage monitoring to ensure you get the price the model intended, not the price you settle for.

How it works

The pipeline is event-driven: when the signal engine emits a trade recommendation, it passes through a pre-trade risk check (position limits, concentration limits, margin requirements) in under 5ms. If it clears, the order is routed to the optimal venue using a smart order router that considers spread, depth, and recent fill rates. Large orders are split using VWAP or TWAP algorithms to minimize market impact. Post-trade, the system records the execution quality — comparing the actual fill price against the signal price — so you always know exactly how much implementation cost you. In simple terms: it makes sure the gap between "what the model wanted" and "what actually happened" is as small as physically possible.

Under the Hood

The Mathematics
Behind Every Decision

Each algorithm is chosen for a specific reason. No filler, no buzzwords — just the methods that institutional risk desks actually use.

Risk

GARCH(1,1) Volatility

Models volatility as a time-varying process: σ²ₜ = ω + α·ε²ₜ₋₁ + β·σ²ₜ₋₁. Captures volatility clustering — the empirical fact that large returns tend to follow large returns.

In plain English: Markets don't have constant risk. After a big move, the next few days tend to be more volatile too. GARCH measures this "momentum of risk" so the system knows when to tighten position sizes.

Risk

Monte Carlo VaR & CVaR

Generates thousands of correlated random scenarios from the estimated return distribution. VaR = quantile at confidence level α. CVaR = E[Loss | Loss > VaR], the conditional expectation in the tail.

In plain English: Instead of guessing the worst case, the system simulates 10,000 possible tomorrows and measures how bad the worst ones get. VaR tells you the boundary of normal bad days. CVaR tells you how bad it gets when things go really wrong.

Covariance

Ledoit-Wolf Shrinkage

Optimally blends the sample covariance matrix with a structured target (e.g., constant correlation) to minimize estimation error. Shrinkage intensity is computed analytically, not tuned.

In plain English: When you have many assets, the raw correlation estimates are noisy and unreliable. Ledoit-Wolf "calms them down" by blending them with a simpler model, producing a covariance matrix that doesn't cause the optimizer to make absurd bets on estimation noise.

Signal

Hidden Markov Model

A probabilistic model with latent states (Bull / Bear / Sideways). Transition probabilities and emission distributions are fitted via the Baum-Welch algorithm. Viterbi decoding identifies the most likely current state.

In plain English: Markets cycle through phases — trending up, trending down, or going nowhere. The HMM figures out which phase we're in right now, even though the phase itself is invisible. It's like detecting the season by looking at the weather.

Optimization

CVaR Portfolio Optimization

Minimizes Conditional Value at Risk subject to return targets and position constraints. Formulated as a linear program via the Rockafellar-Uryasev transformation. Coherent risk measure.

In plain English: Traditional portfolio optimization treats good surprises and bad surprises the same. CVaR optimization only cares about the bad ones. It builds a portfolio that specifically protects against the worst-case scenarios while still targeting your return goal.

Validation

Kupiec & Christoffersen Tests

Kupiec: likelihood-ratio test of unconditional VaR coverage. Christoffersen: tests both coverage and independence of exceptions. Rejects models that produce clustered breaches.

In plain English: A risk model is useless if it's wrong. These tests check whether the model's predictions actually match reality. If the model says "you'll lose more than ₹X only 1% of the time" — these tests verify that it really is 1%, and that the bad days aren't clumping together.
